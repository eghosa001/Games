# RENEW — Multi-Region World Layer

The macro map now sits above the existing district system. Districts are local neighborhoods; regions are the larger economic territories an expanding company competes across.

## Regions

| Region | Unlock REP | Advantage | Main industry |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Old Market Region | 0 | Consumer trade hub | Consumer Goods |
| Industrial Belt Region | 15 | Factory efficiency | Building Materials |
| River Port Region | 30 | Import/export gateway | Consumer Goods |
| Agricultural Plains | 35 | Food supply abundance | Food Processing |
| Northern Growth Corridor | 50 | Fast-growing market | Consumer Goods |
| Resource Basin | 70 | Cheap raw resources | Building Materials |

Every region has demand, market growth, logistics cost, labor cost, competition, rival presence, a resource specialization and infrastructure levels.

## Player expansion

- F3/F4: cycle unlocked regions
- F6: establish a regional operation
- F7: upgrade regional infrastructure (up to level 3)
- F8: dispatch 10 finished goods from the starter region to the selected region

Established regions generate recurring regional revenue. Better infrastructure increases revenue and reduces effective logistics cost.

## Dynamic competition

Regional markets grow over time. Rival pressure can increase, creating a reason to establish a presence before competitors dominate a promising territory.

The intended strategic decision is no longer simply "buy the next business." The player chooses **where** the next business should exist, balancing demand, labor, logistics, competition and specialization.

## Next layer

The next iteration should connect regional presence to full branch-level businesses, region-specific resource extraction, cross-region supply contracts and rival corporate expansion budgets. This layer is intentionally implemented first as a lightweight macro economy so the core restoration/business loop remains understandable.
